Win-Win Fractional CMO Services is designed to run marketing end to end for sales-led B2B companies that win business through relationships. Your Win-Win Marketing Leader sets the strategy with your sales team, gets the work done, and measures how well it supports sales.

For a professional employer organization (PEO), a referral from an accountant can open the door to a conversation about outsourcing payroll and HR. A buyer evaluating a human capital management (HCM) platform might meet a rep at an HR conference and look up how the platform handles payroll for employees in multiple states. In both cases, marketing gives the buyer a way to learn about you before the first call and something they can share when they bring colleagues into the decision.

That takes close coordination with sales. In [Gartner’s survey of 632 B2B buyers](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-sales-survey-finds-61-percent-of-b2b-buyers-prefer-a-rep-free-buying-experience), 69% reported that information from sales reps differed from what they found on the company’s website. Buyers generally preferred to do their own research, but they wanted input from a rep when deciding whether the offering fit their business. A large marketing department can lose touch with what buyers are asking when it plans campaigns on its own. Win-Win gives you a seasoned marketing leader who works directly with your reps and builds the [marketing formula](https://winwincmo.com/blog/marketing-formula-relationship-based-sales/) around how they win business.

**Your sales team builds the relationship. Win-Win gets answers into the buyer’s hands, even when the rep isn’t in the room.**

## Every channel has a job in the relationship

Before a buyer reaches out, they may look you up after a referral. Your website can answer the questions they’re already asking. Search and AI discoverability make those answers easier to find. For businesses that serve a defined region, consistent business listings can make it easier for buyers to tell where you work and how to reach you.

A trade show, a LinkedIn conversation, or an introduction from an industry partner can give a rep a reason to start a conversation. Paid media can put a relevant case study or answer in front of the accounts your reps are pursuing. A trade publication can give those buyers a chance to hear how a customer handled a similar decision.

Say an HR director meets your PEO rep at a conference. On the first call, she asks whether her employees will have to do anything differently to get paid after the switch. Your website outlines what to expect when moving to a PEO. After the call, your rep sends a transition guide the HR director can forward to her payroll manager. It shows what payroll records the PEO needs and who checks the first payroll before it runs. When the payroll manager joins the next call, she can ask a more specific question. Can we review a sample payroll register before we approve the first run?

Buyers may also take those questions to an AI tool as they compare providers. You can see what they’re likely to find by trying these [five AI search prompts for B2B sales teams](https://winwincmo.com/blog/ai-search-after-the-sales-call/).

## How we measure the health of your relationship support channels

Your dashboard shows how your brand measures up against competitors and how the channels you’ve chosen are supporting sales. We start with a baseline and look at changes in the context of your buying season.

- **Brand clarity and consistency.** Your messaging grid gives us a way to check whether your website and sales materials communicate the same value to each buyer. We also look at whether business listings describe your company consistently and how your brand shows up alongside competitors in search and AI answers.
- **Channel contribution.** We measure each channel against its job. For a trade show, that could mean tracking conversations that turn into follow-up meetings. For paid media, we can compare spend with the number of qualified leads it generates when the data is available. For a guide, we ask reps when they send it and what buyers do with it.
- **Lead quality and handoff.** Where the CRM supports it, we track marketing-qualified leads (MQLs) and how many become sales-qualified leads (SQLs). We agree with sales on what qualifies a lead at each stage so the dashboard shows whether marketing is bringing in people sales can have a productive conversation with.

If a trade show gives reps strong conversations and few follow-up meetings, we look at what happens in the days after the event. A better follow-up might be a payroll transition guide the HR director can forward to the payroll manager before the next call. The dashboard tells us where to look. Sales tells us what buyers still need.
