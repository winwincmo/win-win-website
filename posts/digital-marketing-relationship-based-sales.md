If your business grows through relationships, you know the importance of showing up where those relationships develop. Industry conferences and professional associations create opportunities to meet prospective customers and earn their trust.

But what happens between those conversations?

Your prospects are researching solutions and comparing providers, often without involving your sales team. And your competitors are influencing those same decisions.

According to [McKinsey's 2026 Global B2B Pulse Survey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/the-surprising-economics-of-B2B-growth-the-new-survival-threshold-and-what-it-takes-to-thrive), B2B buyers use an average of ten channels throughout their purchasing journey. Meanwhile, [Gartner's 2026 research](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-20-gartner-survey-finds-sixty-nine-percent-of-b-two-b-buyers-turn-to-sales-reps-to-validate-ai-generated-insights) found that 69% prefer to validate AI-generated insights with a sales representative.

As digital marketing becomes more conversational, buyers can ask detailed questions about their options and expect relevant answers before speaking with anyone.

**Your salespeople build relationships and earn trust. Marketing nurtures those relationships as they develop, keeping your strengths top of mind and giving buyers the information they need to move forward with confidence.**

An orchestrated messaging strategy makes that possible. It anticipates buyer needs and addresses concerns that could prevent customers from choosing you.

Three digital strategies are particularly valuable: AI visibility, remarketing, and CRM-driven communications.

## Start with a deep understanding of your ideal customer

Before investing in channels, understand your ideal customer profile (ICP). What pressures influence their decisions? What problems are they trying to solve? How do they describe those challenges in their own words?

Your sales team already has valuable insight. They know why customers hesitate, which objections arise during competitive evaluations, and what makes your company a strong choice.

That knowledge should shape your messaging.

Consider a specialty insurance broker. A retail agent struggling with a difficult casualty placement needs confidence that someone understands the exposure and can find a workable solution. Generic promises of exceptional service won't address that concern.

As the relationship develops, the buyer's questions change. They may begin by evaluating your expertise, then look for evidence of results or reassurance about implementation.

Your messaging strategy should anticipate those questions and make useful answers available throughout the buying process.

## 1. AI visibility: Make your expertise part of the buyer's research

Imagine a prospect asks ChatGPT to compare your company with two competitors.

Does the response recognize your expertise and explain what distinguishes your approach? Or does a competitor receive a more compelling introduction?

Your company could have decades of experience and an excellent reputation yet remain poorly represented in AI-generated answers.

Gartner's 2026 research found that 45% of surveyed B2B buyers used generative AI during a recent purchase, primarily to research vendors and products.

That creates a competitive opportunity for companies willing to make their expertise more discoverable.

Start by asking the questions your ICP would ask when evaluating providers. Compare how your company and competitors appear in the answers.

You may find that competitors are recognized for capabilities your company also offers. Perhaps your strongest customer success stories haven't been published, or your website doesn't clearly explain an important specialization.

Those gaps should inform your content strategy.

Specific service descriptions and useful articles can establish your expertise. Case studies can provide evidence and address common objections.

[Google's guidance on AI search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) emphasizes useful content and established SEO practices. While AI recommendations can't be guaranteed, credible information improves the foundation these systems have for representing your business.

The opportunity is to make sure buyers recognize your strengths when they're deciding which companies deserve consideration.

## 2. Remarketing: Keep your strengths top of mind

A prospect meets your sales leader at a conference, has a promising conversation, and visits your website afterward.

They're interested, but not ready to buy.

Remarketing can reinforce your company's expertise as they continue researching options. It allows you to reach eligible audiences that have previously interacted with your business through relevant digital advertising.

The value comes from choosing messages that address the buyer's concerns.

Someone researching your specialized services might see a case study demonstrating how your team solved a similar problem. A prospect comparing providers could encounter content explaining the business value of your approach.

Instead of repeatedly promoting your company, you're giving buyers useful information that builds confidence.

Remarketing requires appropriate audience permissions and frequency controls. But when the messaging is relevant, it can maintain familiarity throughout a long sales cycle.

The opportunity is to reinforce what distinguishes your business and address questions that could slow a purchasing decision.

## 3. CRM communications: Care for the relationship as it develops

Your CRM contains valuable information about the relationships you've established. It can reveal what customers need and where prospective buyers are in their decision-making process.

Use that knowledge to plan communications that offer value at each stage.

A new prospect may appreciate content that clarifies a business challenge. Someone evaluating providers might benefit from a relevant case study. An established customer may welcome advice that makes their work easier or an introduction to a service that solves a new problem.

Each communication should reinforce why your company is valuable to that person.

### Make networking follow-up worth receiving

Think about how many people your prospects meet at industry conferences. They exchange contact information and return to an inbox full of messages saying, "It was great meeting you. Let's stay in touch."

Most are quickly forgotten.

A more meaningful follow-up reflects something you learned during the conversation.

If someone mentioned a challenge with their current provider, share an article that addresses the issue. You might introduce them to someone with relevant expertise or offer an insight they can apply to their business.

Your CRM can capture those details so future communications reflect the person's interests.

And you shouldn't have to wait until next year's conference to reconnect. Relevant email communications and occasional personal outreach can continue adding value between events.

Automation can support the process, but thoughtful planning matters more than frequency. You need to understand what each audience values and how those needs change.

**People remember the companies that understand their business and have something useful to say.**

## One Messaging Strategy Across Channels

Imagine your sales team identifies a recurring objection that's slowing purchasing decisions.

Marketing develops content that addresses the concern in the customer's language and demonstrates your company's expertise.

That content can make your business more discoverable through AI-powered research. It can support remarketing campaigns reaching interested prospects and become part of CRM communications with contacts facing similar challenges.

Your salespeople can also use it in their conversations.

One strong piece of content can reinforce your competitive strengths across several points in the buying process.

This is the value of an orchestrated messaging strategy. You anticipate what buyers need and make relevant information available wherever they're engaging with your company.

AI-supported marketing operations make this increasingly practical for lean teams, allowing content and customer insights to be shared across channels.

## Measure what moves relationships forward

For relationship-driven businesses, marketing should be measured against its contribution to purchasing decisions and revenue.

AI visibility can be evaluated through relevant buyer queries and competitive comparisons. Remarketing can be assessed through qualified engagement and pipeline contribution. CRM communications can be measured through customer engagement and opportunities developing from existing relationships.

I'd also ask the sales team whether prospects arrive better informed and whether common objections are becoming easier to address.

Those insights matter, even when every interaction can't be attributed to a particular campaign.

## Give buyers more reasons to choose you

Relationship-based sales will continue to depend on people who understand customers and earn their trust.

Marketing has an important role in caring for those relationships as they develop.

As digital marketing becomes more conversational, an orchestrated messaging strategy can keep your strengths top of mind, anticipate buyers' next questions, and address concerns before they become reasons to choose a competitor.

AI visibility, remarketing, and CRM communications extend that strategy throughout the buying journey.

**Your salespeople build the relationships. Your marketing keeps demonstrating why those relationships are worth having.**
