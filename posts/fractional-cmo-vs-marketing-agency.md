**A marketing agency is generally hired to deliver marketing services. A fractional CMO is hired to lead the marketing function and take responsibility for how those services contribute to business growth.** The better choice depends on whether your company needs more execution capacity, stronger marketing leadership, or a combination of both.

Of course, the lines aren't absolute. Some agencies offer excellent strategic leadership. Some fractional CMOs take an active role in execution. The important question is who will make the decisions and remain accountable for the overall marketing investment.

If you're leading a growing B2B company, you've probably reached a point where marketing needs more attention. Your salespeople are building relationships and bringing in opportunities. They also need stronger materials. The website could communicate your value more clearly. You'd like to generate more interest from prospective customers, but you aren't sure which investment will make the greatest difference.

At this point, it makes sense to explore both a marketing agency and fractional CMO services, weighing the advantages of each for your business.

## Fractional CMO vs. marketing agency: what's the difference?

| Consideration | Marketing agency | Fractional CMO |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary role | Delivers agreed marketing services and campaigns | Leads marketing strategy and directs the overall function |
| Accountability | Typically accountable for the work and results defined in its scope | Accountable for marketing priorities, investment decisions and agreed business outcomes |
| Common responsibilities | Creative production, websites, content, media or specialized campaigns | Positioning, budgets, sales alignment, team direction and performance reviews |
| Working relationship | Usually operates as an external services partner | Typically works with the CEO and other business leaders as part of the leadership team |
| Best fit | The strategy is clear, but execution requires more capacity or expertise | Marketing needs senior direction or lacks a clear connection to revenue |
| Can they work together? | Yes. An agency can provide execution within a broader strategy | Yes. A fractional CMO can select and manage agency partners |

The comparison is about the roles companies most often buy, not a rule about what any individual agency or fractional leader can offer. I'd pay close attention to the actual scope of responsibility in either engagement.

## When should you hire a marketing agency?

An agency often makes sense when you know what needs to happen and need experienced people to get the work done.

You might have an established marketing leader who wants to rebuild the website. Or perhaps your company has a clear growth plan and needs a partner to manage paid media. An agency can provide specialists without requiring permanent hires for every skill.

A good agency can contribute ideas beyond the immediate brief. Many agencies have strategists who understand the customer's business quite well. What matters is whether the agreement gives that team responsibility for the entire marketing function or for a defined part of it.

If the CEO is still deciding which campaigns to fund and why, hiring more execution capacity may leave the central leadership question unresolved.

## When should you hire a fractional CMO?

A fractional chief marketing officer provides senior marketing leadership without requiring a full-time executive hire. The person works within an agreed scope and level of involvement, with responsibility for the direction of marketing.

That begins with understanding how your business actually wins customers. A B2B company built on referrals and established sales relationships may need a very different program from one built around high-volume digital acquisition.

The fractional CMO should establish priorities with leadership, align the plan with sales and build a budget that reflects the company's goals. The role also includes evaluating what the current team and partners are producing, so marketing isn't measured solely by how much activity it generates.

In my experience, businesses sometimes have capable employees and good outside partners, yet still struggle to make the pieces work together. The missing investment may be someone with the authority and experience to decide what matters most.

## Can a fractional CMO and a marketing agency work together?

Absolutely. A fractional CMO can give an agency the strategic direction it needs and ensure the work supports the business's sales process.

The CMO may establish positioning and campaign priorities, then engage an agency for specialized production or channel management. A marketing employee might coordinate day-to-day activity internally. The arrangement depends on the amount of work and the capabilities already available.

Clear ownership makes the partnership stronger. The agency should know what success looks like within its scope. The CMO should be responsible for the broader decisions about priorities and investment.

For an established B2B company, that can be a practical way to access experienced leadership and specialized execution without automatically building a large permanent department.

## Is a fractional CMO less expensive than a marketing agency?

It's worth separating the cost of leadership from the cost of doing the marketing work.

A fractional CMO fee pays for agreed leadership responsibilities. An agency fee pays for agreed services and deliverables. Either engagement may also require a separate budget for advertising, events or production. Comparing monthly fees without comparing scopes can be misleading.

A fractional arrangement can reduce the fixed cost of obtaining senior leadership compared with hiring a full-time executive. But a fractional CMO does not automatically replace the need for designers, writers or other specialists. An agency may be a more economical choice when the business already has sound leadership and needs a specific capability.

The opportunity I'm most interested in is a **leaner marketing operation with experienced senior leadership**, supported by the right people and AI-enabled workflows. That model can keep more of the budget available for the initiatives that are most relevant to revenue.

## Why does the sales relationship matter so much in B2B marketing?

For relationship-driven businesses, marketing should strengthen the trust the sales organization has earned. That means providing useful information before a prospect calls and reinforcing the company's expertise after the relationship begins.

The [2026 6sense B2B Buyer Experience Report](https://6sense.com/buyer-experience-report-2026) found that **91.4% of buying groups had ranked their vendor shortlist before contacting sellers**. The vendor favored before contact ultimately won about **82% of the time**.

Those findings suggest that a business can't rely entirely on sales conversations to shape how buyers understand its value. Prospective customers are forming preferences long before they speak to someone.

Your salespeople understand what customers ask and which concerns affect a decision. Marketing can make that knowledge more accessible through the website and useful sales content. It can also give buyers a clearer picture of the company before the first conversation.

## How could agentic AI change this decision in 2027?

One of the developments I'll be watching in 2027 is whether companies begin to gain greater efficiency from coordinated AI operations than from employees using AI independently.

Today, one person might use AI for account research while another uses it for content. Both work faster, but the understanding they develop may remain scattered across separate tools and conversations.

Imagine instead a marketing operation where authorized employees and AI agents work from a shared, carefully maintained knowledge base. It contains approved positioning, customer insights and what the sales organization has learned over time. An agent preparing content could draw on the same understanding as an agent supporting a campaign. New information could be checked and added for future work.

The potential advantage is continuity. A connected AI workforce could have access to more relevant institutional knowledge than any one employee can readily recall. It could reduce the time spent repeatedly gathering context and make the work more consistent.

There's evidence that improving individual productivity alone isn't enough. In [McKinsey's October 2026 analysis of AI and organizational design](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/people-and-organization/our-insights/ai-is-changing-work-now-it-has-to-change-the-organization), **80% of respondents** reported improved individual productivity from AI, but only **37%** reported a positive impact on operating earnings. Nearly three-quarters of AI high performers reported substantially redesigning their workflows, compared with about one-quarter of other organizations.

[Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization) found that only **19% of surveyed AI users** had both strong individual capabilities and organizational conditions that reinforced their use. Meanwhile, [Gartner's 2026 CMO Spend Survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-2026-cmo-spend-survey-finds-cmos-allocate-15-point-3-percent-of-marketing-budgets-to-ai-but-only-30-percent-are-ready-to-scale-ai-capabilities) found that CMOs allocated an average of **15.3% of marketing budgets to AI**, while only **30%** reported mature or fully developed AI readiness. Gartner's respondents were predominantly from large enterprises, so those figures shouldn't be treated as benchmarks for every smaller B2B business.

The opportunity is promising, but it comes with obligations. Shared information needs to be accurate and current. Customer data needs appropriate access controls. Agents also need clear boundaries and human review for consequential decisions.

As AI expands the amount of work smaller teams can manage, experienced marketing leadership may become even more valuable. Someone still needs to decide what deserves the company's resources and how the work contributes to revenue.

## Which option is right for your business?

I would begin with the constraint your company is actually experiencing.

If you have a sound strategy and strong leadership but can't execute quickly enough, an agency may be the right next investment. If marketing activity keeps increasing without a clear connection to sales, a fractional CMO may be the more useful starting point.

If you need both leadership and execution, consider a fractional CMO working alongside an agency or other specialists. The right arrangement should reflect the company's goals and the capabilities it already has.

The decision is less about choosing a marketing label than about putting accountability in the right place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a marketing agency provide the same strategic leadership as a fractional CMO?

Some can. Agencies may offer embedded strategy or outsourced CMO services. Ask who has authority over the overall marketing budget, who coordinates work beyond the agency's scope and who is accountable to the CEO for marketing's contribution to growth.

### Do you need an internal marketing team before hiring a fractional CMO?

No. A fractional CMO can lead an existing team or establish the marketing plan before new resources are added. The amount of execution support required will depend on the work the business needs to accomplish.

### Can a fractional CMO manage an existing agency?

Yes. Managing agency relationships and ensuring their work supports the broader marketing strategy can be part of a fractional CMO engagement. The responsibilities should be explicit in the agreement.

### Is the agency budget included in a fractional CMO fee?

Not necessarily. Leadership fees, agency services and marketing program expenses may be separate. Ask for a clear accounting of what is included and what requires additional investment.

### Can AI replace a marketing agency or fractional CMO?

AI can take on some research and production tasks, and coordinated agents may change how companies staff marketing operations. It still requires reliable source information, oversight and strategic decisions. The amount of human and external support a company needs will vary.

## Build the marketing function around how your business grows

For years, businesses have expanded marketing by adding services and people as needs arose. AI offers an opportunity to rethink the structure, but the principle behind a sound marketing investment hasn't changed.

Start with how customers choose your business. Establish who is accountable for marketing decisions. Then invest in the capabilities that will make the greatest contribution.
